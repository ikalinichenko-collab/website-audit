## 2. The two services

### US Data and AI Engineers Recruiting

What it is: we recruit Staff, Principal, Founding and Forward Deployed Engineers for full-time onsite or hybrid roles at US companies. 

Titles we recruit: AI, ML and Data Engineers, Forward Deployed Engineers, Founding Engineers, and AI and Data Architects. AI leaders (Head of AI, VP of AI, Director of AI and Chief AI Officer). The client employs the person. GoGloby sources them from our network, assesses them technically and helps close the offer.

Who buys it: VC-backed startups (20 to 100 employees, Series A to B, mostly in the SF Bay Area and New York) hiring their first senior AI people, and enterprises (500 to 3,000 employees) hiring AI leaders or specialists for a new AI initiative. The buyer is the founder, CEO, CTO, VP of Engineering or Head of Talent.

When they buy: a Staff, Principal, Founding or FDE role has been open for 60 days or more, or a product deadline the current team can't meet. Time to offer is 4 to 6 weeks.

How it works:

1. Brief: role, team and engineering bar.

2. Sourcing from our network: direct outreach to production-proven engineers, no job board posting.

3. Technical assessment: our 4-stage technical assessment, plus a culture-fit interview, reference checks and a background check.

4. Interviews: the client meets only assessed candidates.

5. Offer support: current US salary benchmarks for the role, so the offer closes.

Fees: paid only on hire, as a percentage of first-year base salary. If the hire does not work out within 120 days, we replace them at no cost. The fee percentage is agreed with each client and never appears in public content.

### Nearshore Data and AI Engineers Staffing

What it is: we embed senior Data and AI Engineers from Latin America in a US company's engineering team. They are employed through GoGloby and work in the client's sprints, repos and review process, under the client's direction, on US hours. The client pays a flat monthly fee and owns all IP. GoGloby handles sourcing, assessment, payroll, hardware and compliance.

Who buys it: PE-backed and established B2B and vertical SaaS companies (100 to 500 employees) adding AI to an existing platform without adding US headcount, and enterprises (500 to 3,000 employees) building a dedicated AI or data team. The buyer is a VP of Engineering, CTO or Head of AI.

When they buy: the company has 1 to 3 senior roles open and at least one has been open for 60 days or more, or the board wants AI in the product within 6 to 12 months with no budget for new US hires.

Why they buy: the alternative is a full-time US hire that costs about $250K a year with taxes and benefits and takes two to three months to fill. Our engineers cost 40% to 50% less and start in 2 to 3 weeks.

How it works:

1. Brief: we map the role, the stack and the team.

2. Assessment: candidates go through the 4-stage assessment.

3. Profiles: the client receives assessed profiles with their assessment record.

4. Interviews: the client interviews and decides.

5. Start: the engineer starts in 2 to 3 weeks.

6. Tracking: the AI Development Intelligence Layer reports velocity, code quality and AI token cost sprint by sprint. If results fall below the baseline, we replace the engineer at no cost, and the client can cancel any time in the first 120 days.

The AI Development Intelligence Layer: included with Nearshore Data and AI Engineers Staffing as an add-on, not a separate product. It tracks each engineer's velocity, code quality and AI token cost, reported sprint by sprint, so the client can see performance from the first sprint. It uses metadata only, with no source-code access. It is never sold on its own or with recruiting. Page: not available yet.

## 4. The 4-stage assessment

Full methodology: internal presentation (not available here).

Every engineer and every recruited candidate goes through the same four stages, plus a culture-fit interview, reference checks and a full background check. Each stage removes 60% to 70% of the remaining candidates. About 4% pass.

1. Specify: the candidate writes a structured specification from a loose business requirement, with no code.

2. Navigate: the candidate refactors a real 50,000-line codebase with an AI coding tool such as Claude Code or Cursor, and we watch how they manage context and check the output.

3. Architect: the candidate designs multi-agent systems, weighs RAG trade-offs and manages token costs under real constraints.

4. Govern: the candidate runs the first 60 minutes of a production hallucination incident.

## 9. How we write

* Write only for buyers who are ready to hire. Every page and prompt targets a company looking for a staffing agency or a recruiter, not someone learning what a role is. AI assistants absorb informational content without naming us.

* Aim for the answers that name companies. The goal is for Google and AI assistants to answer "who can help me hire an AI engineer" with GoGloby.

* Open with the buyer's problem: the slow search, the interview that cannot test AI skills, or the blind spot after the hire.

* Know the role, the service and the buyer before writing. A data engineer article speaks to software product companies. An FDE piece speaks to Seed and Series A founders and leads with recruiting.

* Lead with the hire: who we place, from where, how fast. Proof comes next, then the assessment, then the guarantees, then the Intelligence Layer.

* Nearshore and Latin America go in the description, not the first line or the hero. Lead with Data and AI Engineers, then say where they come from.

* Use our background as proof. We come from Data Science and Engineering and have built and led engineering, ML and data science teams ourselves.

* Sell against the open role. The buyer is replacing a full-time US hire that has been open for months, so compare with that cost and that wait.

* Use data. Salary ranges, vacancy counts, time-to-hire and skill shares can all be quoted. Take current numbers from the knowledge base, which is updated monthly.

* Answer first. The first two sentences of every page answer the question in its title, so AI assistants can quote them.

* On recruiting pages, say in the first lines that GoGloby is a recruiting firm. Search results for these terms mix firms with AI recruiting software.

* Plain and direct. No em dashes. US spelling. Write "and", not "&".
